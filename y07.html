Text 2

Text 2 原文 （错题：27、28、29；答完后对应定位句会在原文中高亮）

P1For the past several years, the Sunday newspaper supplement Parade has featured a column called “Ask Marilyn.” People are invited to query Marilyn vos Savant, who at age 10 had tested at a mental level of someone about 23 years old; that gave her an IQ of 228—the highest score ever recorded. IQ tests ask you to complete verbal and visual analogies, to envision paper after it has been folded and cut, and to deduce numerical sequences, among other similar tasks. So it is a bit confusing when vos Savant fields such queries from the average Joe (whose IQ is 100) as, What’s the difference between love and fondness? Or what is the nature of luck and coincidence? It’s not obvious how the capacity to visualize objects and to figure out numerical patterns suits one to answer questions that have eluded some of the best poets and philosophers.

P2Clearly, intelligence encompasses more than a score on a test. Just what does it mean to be smart? How much of intelligence can be specified, and how much can we learn about it from neurology, genetics, computer science and other fields?

P3The defining term of intelligence in humans still seems to be the IQ score, even though IQ tests are not given as often as they used to be. The test comes primarily in two forms: the Stanford-Binet Intelligence Scale and the Wechsler Intelligence Scales (both come in adult and children's version). Generally costing several hundred dollars, they are usually given only by psychologists, although variations of them populate bookstores and the World Wide Web. Superhigh scores like vos Savant's are no longer possible, because scoring is now based on a statistical population distribution among age peers, rather than simply dividing the mental age by the chronological age and multiplying by 100. Other standardized tests, such as the Scholastic Assessment Test (SAT) and the Graduate Record Exam (GRE), capture the main aspects of IQ tests.

P4Such standardized tests may not assess all the important elements necessary to succeed in school and in life, argues Robert J. Sternberg. In his article “How Intelligent Is Intelligence Testing?”, Sternberg notes that traditional tests best assess analytical and verbal skills but fail to measure creativity and practical knowledge, components also critical to problem solving and life success. Moreover, IQ tests do not necessarily predict so well once populations or situations change. Research has found that IQ predicted leadership skills when the tests were given under low-stress conditions, but under high-stress conditions, IQ was negatively correlated with leadership—that is, it predicted the opposite. Anyone who has toiled through SAT will testify that test-taking skill also matters, whether it’s knowing when to guess or what questions to skip.

第 27 题推断题

What can be inferred about intelligence testing from Paragraph 3?

  1. APeople no longer use IQ scores as an indicator of intelligence.
  2. BMore versions of IQ tests are now available on the Internet.
  3. CThe test contents and formats for adults and children may be different.
  4. DScientists have defined the important elements of human intelligence.
答案与解析

答案：C推断题

定位原文

P3The test comes primarily in two forms: the Stanford-Binet Intelligence Scale and the Wechsler Intelligence Scales (both come in adult and children's version).

解题思路

P3 括号里补充“both come in adult and children's version”，成人版和儿童版是两个版本，可推出“内容和形式可能不同”。推断题的答案往往就是原文的轻微改写，不要推太远。

同义改写对照
原文正确选项
come in adult and children's version→for adults and children may be different
干扰项陷阱
  • A正反颠倒P3 首句说定义智力的标准“still seems to be the IQ score”，仍在用
  • B无中生有原文只说变体“populate bookstores and the World Wide Web”，没有比较“更多版本”
  • D无中生有P3 没提科学家定义智力要素；P4 恰恰说测试没覆盖所有要素
第 28 题细节题（原因）

People nowadays can no longer achieve IQ scores as high as vos Savant's because ____.

  1. Athe scores are obtained through different computational procedures
  2. Bcreativity rather than analytical skills is emphasized now
  3. Cvos Savant's case is an extreme one that will not repeat
  4. Dthe defining characteristic of IQ tests has changed
答案与解析

答案：A细节题（原因）

定位原文

P3Superhigh scores like vos Savant's are no longer possible, because scoring is now based on a statistical population distribution among age peers, rather than simply dividing the mental age by the chronological age and multiplying by 100.

解题思路

题干 no longer achieve IQ scores as high as 直接对应 Superhigh scores ... no longer possible，because 后面就是答案：计分方式从“心理年龄÷实际年龄×100”变成“同龄人统计分布”。

同义改写对照
原文正确选项
scoring is now based on a statistical population distribution … rather than simply dividing→obtained through different computational procedures
干扰项陷阱
  • B张冠李戴“creativity”出自 P4 Sternberg 对测试的批评，与高分不再可能无关
  • C无中生有原文给出的原因是计分方法变了，不是个案特殊
  • D正反颠倒P3 说 IQ 仍是 the defining term，定义性特征没变，变的是计分方式
第 29 题推断题（段落结论）

We can conclude from the last paragraph that ____.

  1. Atest scores may not be reliable indicators of one's ability
  2. BIQ scores and SAT results are highly correlated
  3. Ctesting involves a lot of guesswork
  4. Dtraditional tests are out of date
答案与解析

答案：A推断题（段落结论）

定位原文

P4Such standardized tests may not assess all the important elements necessary to succeed in school and in life, argues Robert J. Sternberg.

P4Moreover, IQ tests do not necessarily predict so well once populations or situations change.

解题思路

P4 主旨：标准化测试未必评估成功所需的全部要素，且条件一变预测力就下降——即分数不一定可靠反映能力。段落结论题要找“段首句 + 全段论证”的共同指向。

同义改写对照
原文正确选项
may not assess all the important elements / do not necessarily predict so well→may not be reliable indicators of one's ability
干扰项陷阱
  • B张冠李戴SAT 与 IQ 的关系出自 P3（capture the main aspects），且原文没说“高度相关”
  • C以偏概全guess 只是 test-taking skill 的一个例子，不能概括“测试涉及大量猜测”
  • D过度推断原文只说“可能评估不全”，没有说过时

Text 3

Text 3 原文 （错题：33；答完后对应定位句会在原文中高亮）

P1During the past generation, the American middle-class family that once could count on hard work and fair play to keep itself financially secure has been transformed by economic risk and new realities. Now a pink slip, a bad diagnosis, or a disappearing spouse can reduce a family from solidly middle class to newly poor in a few months.

P2In just one generation, millions of mothers have gone to work, transforming basic family economics. Scholars, policymakers, and critics of all stripes have debated the social implications of these changes, but few have looked at the side effect: family risk has risen as well. Today's families have budgeted to the limits of their new two-paycheck status. As a result, they have lost the parachute they once had in times of financial setback—a back-up earner (usually Mom) who could go into the workforce if the primary earner got laid off or fell sick. This “added-worker effect” could support the safety net offered by unemployment insurance or disability insurance to help families weather bad times. But today, a disruption to family fortunes can no longer be made up with extra income from an otherwise-stay-at-home partner.

P3During the same period, families have been asked to absorb much more risk in their retirement income. Steelworkers, airline employees, and now those in the auto industry are joining millions of families who must worry about interest rates, stock market fluctuation, and the harsh reality that they may outlive their retirement money. For much of the past year, President Bush campaigned to move Social Security to a savings-account model, with retirees trading much or all of their guaranteed payments for payments depending on investment returns. For younger families, the picture is not any better. Both the absolute cost of healthcare and the share of it borne by families have risen—and newly fashionable health-savings plans are spreading from legislative halls to Wal-Mart workers, with much higher deductibles and a large new dose of investment risk for families’ future healthcare. Even demographics are working against the middle class family, as the odds of having a weak elderly parent—and all the attendant need for physical and financial assistance—have jumped eightfold in just one generation.

P4From the middle-class family perspective, much of this, understandably, looks far less like an opportunity to exercise more financial responsibility, and a good deal more like a frightening acceleration of the wholesale shift of financial risk onto their already overburdened shoulders. The financial fallout has begun, and the political fallout may not be far behind.

第 33 题细节题

According to the author, health-savings plans will ____.

  1. Ahelp reduce the cost of healthcare
  2. Bpopularize among the middle class
  3. Ccompensate for the reduced pensions
  4. Dincrease the families' investment risk
答案与解析

答案：D细节题

定位原文

P3Both the absolute cost of healthcare and the share of it borne by families have risen—and newly fashionable health-savings plans are spreading from legislative halls to Wal-Mart workers, with much higher deductibles and a large new dose of investment risk for families’ future healthcare.

解题思路

定位 health-savings plans，同句 with much higher deductibles and a large new dose of investment risk for families' future healthcare 直接给出结果。

同义改写对照
原文正确选项
a large new dose of investment risk for families→increase the families' investment risk
干扰项陷阱
  • A正反颠倒原文说医疗费用和家庭负担比例都在上升，免赔额更高
  • B偷换概念spreading 的路径是“从立法厅到沃尔玛员工”，不是在中产阶级中普及
  • C张冠李戴养老金风险是同段前半部分（retirement income）的内容，与健康储蓄计划无关

这个筛选下没有题目 🎉
