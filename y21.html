Text 1

Text 1 原文 （错题：21、22；答完后对应定位句会在原文中高亮）

P1How can Britain's train operators possibly justify yet another increase to rail passenger fares? It has become a grimly reliable annual ritual: every January the cost of travelling by train rises, imposing a significant extra burden on those who have no option but to use the rail network to get to work or otherwise. This year's rise, an average of 2.7 per cent, may be a fraction lower than last year's, but it is still well above the official Consumer Price Index (CPI) measure of inflation.

P2Successive governments have permitted such increases on the grounds that the cost of investing in and running the rail network should be borne by those who use it, rather than the general taxpayer. Why, the argument goes, should a car-driving pensioner from Lincolnshire have to subsidise the daily commute of a stockbroker from Surrey? Equally, there is a sense that the travails of commuters in the South East, many of whom will face among the biggest rises, have received too much attention compared to those who must endure the relatively poor infrastructure of the Midlands and the North.

P3However, over the past 12 months, those commuters have also experienced some of the worst rail strikes in years. It is all very well train operators trumpeting the improvements they are making to the network, but passengers should be able to expect a basic level of service for the substantial sums they are now paying to travel. The responsibility for the latest wave of strikes rests on the unions. However, there is a strong case that those who have been worst affected by industrial action should receive compensation for the disruption they have suffered.

P4The Government has pledged to change the law to introduce a minimum service requirement so that, even when strikes occur, services can continue to operate. This should form part of a wider package of measures to address the long-running problems on Britain's railways. Yes, more investment is needed, but passengers will not be willing to pay more indefinitely if they must also endure cramped, unreliable services, interrupted by regular chaos when timetables are changed, or planned maintenance is managed incompetently. The threat of nationalisation may have been seen off for now, but it will return with a vengeance if the justified anger of passengers is not addressed in short order.

第 21 题推断题（观点）

The author holds that this year's increase in rail passenger fares ____.

  1. Awill ease train operators' burden
  2. Bhas kept pace with inflation
  3. Cis a big surprise to commuters
  4. Dremains an unreasonable measure
答案与解析

答案：D推断题（观点）

定位原文

P1How can Britain's train operators possibly justify yet another increase to rail passenger fares? It has become a grimly reliable annual ritual: every January the cost of travelling by train rises, imposing a significant extra burden on those who have no option but to use the rail network … This year's rise, an average of 2.7 per cent, may be a fraction lower than last year's, but it is still well above the official Consumer Price Index (CPI) measure of inflation.

解题思路

开篇反问句 How can … possibly justify yet another increase…? 的真实含义是“根本无法证明其合理”。yet another（又一次）、grimly reliable annual ritual（每年必来的糟心惯例）都带有明显的不满。

虽然今年涨幅比去年略低（may be a fraction lower，让步），但 but 之后强调仍远高于 CPI 通胀——作者认为涨价依旧不合理。remains 对应“又一次”“每年都涨”。

同义改写对照
原文正确选项
How can … possibly justify yet another increase→remains an unreasonable measure
干扰项陷阱
  • A张冠李戴原文是涨价给乘客 imposing a significant extra burden，负担在乘客身上，不是减轻运营商负担
  • B正反颠倒still well above the CPI，远高于通胀而非与通胀持平
  • C正反颠倒grimly reliable annual ritual，每年一月都涨，完全在意料之中
第 22 题推断题（指代）

The stockbroker in Paragraph 2 is used to stand for ____.

  1. Acar drivers
  2. Brail travellers
  3. Clocal investors
  4. Dordinary taxpayers
答案与解析

答案：B推断题（指代）

定位原文

P2Successive governments have permitted such increases on the grounds that the cost of investing in and running the rail network should be borne by those who use it, rather than the general taxpayer. Why, the argument goes, should a car-driving pensioner from Lincolnshire have to subsidise the daily commute of a stockbroker from Surrey?

解题思路

前句给出政府的逻辑：铁路成本应由使用者（those who use it）而非普通纳税人（the general taxpayer）承担。

后句用一组对比形象化：林肯郡开车的退休老人（不坐火车、却要交税）凭什么要补贴萨里郡股票经纪人的日常通勤（daily commute，每天坐火车上班）？所以 pensioner ↔ general taxpayer，stockbroker ↔ those who use it，即铁路乘客。

同义改写对照
原文正确选项
those who use it (the rail network) / the daily commute of a stockbroker→rail travellers
干扰项陷阱
  • A张冠李戴car-driving 修饰的是 pensioner，不是 stockbroker
  • C偷换概念只取“股票经纪人”字面身份；这里取其“每天坐火车通勤的人”之意，与投资无关
  • D张冠李戴代表普通纳税人的是开车的 pensioner，两者恰好对立

Text 3

Text 3 原文 （错题：33、34；答完后对应定位句会在原文中高亮）

P1As a historian who's always searching for the text or the image that makes us re-evaluate the past, I've become preoccupied with looking for photographs that show our Victorian ancestors smiling (what better way to shatter the image of 19th-century prudery?). I've found quite a few, and—since I started posting them on Twitter—they have been causing quite a stir. People have been surprised to see evidence that Victorians had fun and could, and did, laugh. They are noting that the Victorians suddenly seem to become more human as the hundred-or-so years that separate us fade away through our common experience of laughter.

P2Of course, I need to concede that my collection of “Smiling Victorians” makes up only a tiny percentage of the vast catalogue of photographic portraiture created between 1840 and 1900, the majority of which show sitters posing miserably and stiffly in front of painted backdrops, or staring absently into the middle distance. How do we explain this trend?

P3During the 1840s and 1850s, in the early days of photography, exposure times were notoriously long: the daguerreotype photographic method (producing an image on a silvered copper plate) could take several minutes to complete, resulting in blurred images as sitters shifted position or adjusted their limbs. The thought of holding a fixed grin as the camera performed its magical duties was too much to contemplate, and so a non-committal blank stare became the norm.

P4But exposure times were much quicker by the 1880s, and the introduction of the Box Brownie and other portable cameras meant that, though slow by today's digital standards, the exposure was almost instantaneous. Spontaneous smiles were relatively easy to capture by the 1890s, so we must look elsewhere for an explanation of why Victorians still hesitated to smile.

P5One explanation might be the loss of dignity displayed through a cheesy grin. "Nature gave us lips to conceal our teeth," ran one popular Victorian saying, alluding to the fact that before the birth of proper dentistry, mouths were often in a shocking state of hygiene. A flashing set of healthy and clean, regular "pearly whites" was a rare sight in Victorian society, the preserve of the super-rich (and even then, dental hygiene was not guaranteed).

P6A toothy grin (especially when there were gaps or blackened teeth) lacked class: drunks, tramps and music hall performers might gurn and grin with a smile as wide as Lewis Carroll's gum-exposing Cheshire Cat, but it was not a becoming look for properly bred persons. Even Mark Twain, a man who enjoyed a hearty laugh, said that when it came to photographic portraits there could be “nothing more damning than a silly, foolish smile fixed forever”.

第 33 题细节题（原因）

What might have kept the Victorians from smiling for pictures in the 1890s?

  1. ATheir unhealthy dental condition.
  2. BTheir inherent social sensitiveness.
  3. CTheir tension before the camera.
  4. DTheir distrust of new inventions.
答案与解析

答案：A细节题（原因）

定位原文

P4Spontaneous smiles were relatively easy to capture by the 1890s, so we must look elsewhere for an explanation of why Victorians still hesitated to smile.

P5One explanation might be the loss of dignity displayed through a cheesy grin. “Nature gave us lips to conceal our teeth,” ran one popular Victorian saying, alluding to the fact that before the birth of proper dentistry, mouths were often in a shocking state of hygiene. A flashing set of healthy and clean, regular “pearly whites” was a rare sight in Victorian society …

解题思路

题干 in the 1890s 定位 P4 末句：到 1890 年代拍下自然的笑容已不难，技术不再是障碍，“必须到别处找原因”——答案在 P5。

P5 给出的解释：咧嘴笑有失尊严，而之所以有失尊严，是因为正规牙科出现之前人们口腔卫生状况极差，一口健康、洁白、整齐的牙齿非常罕见；P6 进一步说露出牙缝或黑牙的笑“没有格调”。文中落到实处的原因就是糟糕的牙齿状况。

同义改写对照
原文正确选项
mouths were often in a shocking state of hygiene / healthy … “pearly whites” was a rare sight→unhealthy dental condition
干扰项陷阱
  • B偷换概念“有失尊严”是由牙齿难看引起的社会观念，文中没说维多利亚人“天生”敏感，inherent 无依据
  • C时间错位P3 讲的是 1840–50 年代曝光时间太长、难以保持笑容，且没说“紧张”；题干问的是 1890 年代
  • D无中生有文中未提对新发明的不信任
第 34 题例证题

Mark Twain is quoted to show that the disapproval of smiles in pictures was ____.

  1. Aa thought-provoking idea
  2. Ba deep-rooted belief
  3. Ca misguided attitude
  4. Da controversial view
答案与解析

答案：B例证题

定位原文

P6A toothy grin … lacked class … it was not a becoming look for properly bred persons. Even Mark Twain, a man who enjoyed a hearty laugh, said that when it came to photographic portraits there could be “nothing more damning than a silly, foolish smile fixed forever”.

解题思路

关键词是 Even：连马克·吐温这样一个爱开怀大笑的人，都认为照片里定格的傻笑最糟糕——说明“照片中不该笑”的观念在当时深入人心，连最不该持这种观点的人也受其影响。

再结合 P5 的 one popular Victorian saying（流行谚语）、P6 的 not a becoming look for properly bred persons，可见这是普遍、根深蒂固的社会观念。

同义改写对照
原文正确选项
Even Mark Twain, a man who enjoyed a hearty laugh …→a deep-rooted belief
干扰项陷阱
  • A无关项引用不是为了说明这个观点发人深省，而是证明它的普遍性
  • C无中生有作者是在解释原因，并未评价这种观念是错误的
  • D正反颠倒Even 表明连爱笑的人都认同，说明是共识而非争议

Text 4

Text 4 原文 （错题：36、37、38、39；答完后对应定位句会在原文中高亮）

P1From the early days of broadband, advocates for consumers and web-based companies worried that the cable and phone companies selling broadband connections had the power and incentive to favor affiliated websites over their rivals'. That's why there has been such a strong demand for rules that would prevent broadband providers from picking winners and losers online, preserving the freedom and innovation that have been the lifeblood of the internet.

P2Yet that demand has been almost impossible to fill—in part because of pushback from broadband providers, anti-regulatory conservatives and the courts. A federal appeals court weighed in again Tuesday, but instead of providing a badly needed resolution, it only prolonged the fight.

P3At issue before the US Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit was the latest take of the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) on net neutrality, adopted on a party-line vote in 2017. The Republican penned order not only eliminated the strict net neutrality rules the FCC had adopted when it had a Democratic majority in 2015, but rejected the commission's authority to require broadband providers to do much of anything. The order also declared that state and local governments couldn't regulate broadband providers either. The commission argued that other agencies would protect against anti-competitive behavior, such as a broadband-providing conglomerate like AT&T favoring its own video-streaming service at the expense of Netflix and Apple TV. Yet the FCC also ended the investigations of broadband providers that imposed data caps on their rivals' streaming services but not their own.

P4On Tuesday, the appeals court unanimously upheld the 2017 order deregulating broadband providers, citing a Supreme Court ruling from 2005 that upheld a similarly deregulatory move. But Judge Patricia Millett rightly argued in a concurring opinion that “the result is unhinged from the realities of modern broadband service,” and said Congress or the Supreme Court could intervene to “avoid trapping internet regulation in technological anachronism.”

P5In the meantime, the court threw out the FCC’s attempt to block all state rules on net neutrality, while preserving the commission’s power to preempt individual state laws that undermine its order. That means more battles like the one now going on between the Justice Department and California, which enacted a tough net neutrality law in the wake of the FCC’s abdication.

P6The endless legal battles and back-and-forth at the FCC cry out for Congress to act. It needs to give the commission explicit authority once and for all to bar broadband providers from meddling in the traffic on their network and to create clear rules protecting openness and innovation online.

第 36 题细节题

There has long been concern that broadband providers would ____.

  1. Ashow partiality in treating clients
  2. Bbring web-based firms under control
  3. Cslow down the traffic on their network
  4. Dintensify competition with their rivals
答案与解析

答案：A细节题

定位原文

P1From the early days of broadband, advocates for consumers and web-based companies worried that the cable and phone companies selling broadband connections had the power and incentive to favor affiliated websites over their rivals'. That's why there has been such a strong demand for rules that would prevent broadband providers from picking winners and losers online …

解题思路

题干 There has long been concern 对应 From the early days of broadband … worried。

担心的内容：宽带提供商既有能力也有动机偏袒自家关联网站、压制竞争对手的网站；下句 picking winners and losers online（在网上决定谁赢谁输）是同义复现。对作为客户的网站区别对待 = show partiality in treating clients。

同义改写对照
原文正确选项
favor affiliated websites over their rivals' / picking winners and losers→show partiality in treating clients
干扰项陷阱
  • B偷换概念担心的是“偏袒”，不是“控制网络公司”
  • C无中生有文中没说会减慢网速；末段 meddling in the traffic 是“干预流量”，也不是长期担忧的核心
  • D正反颠倒偏袒自家网站恰恰是破坏公平竞争，而非加剧与对手的竞争
第 37 题推断题

Faced with the demand for net neutrality rules, the FCC ____.

  1. Asticks to an out-of-date order
  2. Bhas allowed the states to intervene
  3. Chas issued a special resolution
  4. Dtakes an anti-regulatory stance
答案与解析

答案：D推断题

定位原文

P3The Republican penned order not only eliminated the strict net neutrality rules the FCC had adopted when it had a Democratic majority in 2015, but rejected the commission's authority to require broadband providers to do much of anything. The order also declared that state and local governments couldn't regulate broadband providers either.

P4On Tuesday, the appeals court unanimously upheld the 2017 order deregulating broadband providers …

解题思路

FCC 2017 年的命令不仅废除了 2015 年严格的网络中立规则，还否认自己有权要求宽带商做任何事，甚至宣布州和地方政府也不能监管宽带商。

P4 直接称之为 the 2017 order deregulating broadband providers（放松对宽带商监管的命令），P5 又用 the FCC's abdication（FCC 放弃职责）。可见面对网络中立的呼声，FCC 持反监管立场。

同义改写对照
原文正确选项
eliminated the strict … rules / rejected the commission's authority / deregulating→takes an anti-regulatory stance
干扰项陷阱
  • A张冠李戴“过时”是 Millett 法官对法院判决的批评（technological anachronism）；且 2017 命令本身就是推翻旧规则的新命令
  • B正反颠倒命令明确宣布州和地方政府不得监管宽带商；推翻这一点的是法院
  • C张冠李戴resolution 出现在 P2，说的是法院没能给出亟需的“解决方案”，与 FCC 无关
第 38 题推断题（例证）

What can be learned about AT&T from Paragraph 3?

  1. AIt is in pursuit of quality service.
  2. BIt is under the FCC's investigation.
  3. CIt protects against unfair competition.
  4. DIt engages in anti-competitive practices.
答案与解析

答案：D推断题（例证）

定位原文

P3The commission argued that other agencies would protect against anti-competitive behavior, such as a broadband-providing conglomerate like AT&T favoring its own video-streaming service at the expense of Netflix and Apple TV. Yet the FCC also ended the investigations of broadband providers that imposed data caps on their rivals' streaming services but not their own.

解题思路

AT&T 出现在 such as 引出的例子里，是 anti-competitive behavior（反竞争行为）的例证：像 AT&T 这样的宽带企业集团偏袒自家视频流媒体服务，损害 Netflix 和 Apple TV 的利益。

下一句“只对对手的流媒体设流量上限”也是同类行为。所以 AT&T 从事反竞争行为。

同义改写对照
原文正确选项
anti-competitive behavior, such as … AT&T favoring its own …→engages in anti-competitive practices
干扰项陷阱
  • A无中生有没提追求优质服务
  • B正反颠倒FCC ended the investigations，调查已被终止，且没点名 AT&T
  • C张冠李戴protect against anti-competitive behavior 的主语是 other agencies，AT&T 恰恰是被防范的对象
第 39 题细节题（观点）

Judge Patricia Millett argues that the appeals court's decision ____.

  1. Aconveys an ambiguous message
  2. Bis out of touch with reality
  3. Cis at odds with its earlier rulings
  4. Dfocuses on trivialities
答案与解析

答案：B细节题（观点）

定位原文

P4On Tuesday, the appeals court unanimously upheld the 2017 order …, citing a Supreme Court ruling from 2005 that upheld a similarly deregulatory move. But Judge Patricia Millett rightly argued in a concurring opinion that “the result is unhinged from the realities of modern broadband service,” and said Congress or the Supreme Court could intervene to “avoid trapping internet regulation in technological anachronism.”

解题思路

unhinged from the realities = 与现实脱节。后面 technological anachronism（技术上的时代错误）进一步说明：判决依据的是 2005 年的判例，跟不上现代宽带服务的现实。

同义改写对照
原文正确选项
unhinged from the realities of modern broadband service→out of touch with reality
干扰项陷阱
  • A无中生有判决是 unanimously upheld，立场很明确，没有“含糊”
  • C正反颠倒判决恰恰援引了 2005 年最高法院类似的放松监管判例，与先前裁决一致
  • D无中生有没说判决关注琐事

这个筛选下没有题目 🎉
